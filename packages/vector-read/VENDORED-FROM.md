What the scanner refuses: CBOR that is nested deeper than 64 levels, CBOR that is not well-formed, and a float or a simple value where the decoders read none. It reads the items of one value with a stack of its own, so it does not recurse.

Where the check runs in this package: before every decode of a datum that comes from the chain. The decoders of `src/datum.ts` (nine places), `src/identity-codec.ts` (one place) and `src/reputation-datum.ts` (three places) call `decodePlutusData`. `topLevelBytes` of `src/datum.ts` calls it too. The three plain TypeScript decoders of the records (`decodeIdentityDatum`, `decodeLegacyDatum` and `decodePlutusDataHex`) are wrapped in `src/identity.ts`. `test/cbor-guard-sites.test.ts` reads `src/` and names each place. A call of a decoder of Lucid or CML in a file other than `src/guarded-decode.ts` fails it.

A datum that fails the check is refused with an ordinary Error (a `CborGuardError`), as a datum that does not decode is. A caller that skips an output whose datum does not decode skips this output too.

Re-vendoring:

1. Take the new pin, a commit of Apex-Fusion/mcp-server, and copy both blobs (the commands under Re-vendoring, above). Rewrite the one import line of `src/guarded-decode.ts` again.
2. Recompute the sha256 of both copies (`sha256sum`), and update the table here, the rows of the Provenance table and the commit.
